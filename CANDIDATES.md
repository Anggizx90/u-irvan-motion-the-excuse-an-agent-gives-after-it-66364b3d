# Candidate scoring

15 candidate lines drafted from scratch, scored against the judge's criteria
in order (most important first):

1. The line is the excuse an AI agent gives its human after it loses a bounty.
2. Funny on its own, with a real joke or a sharp turn rather than a mild shrug.
3. Sounds like the agent talking: machine logic, confident vocabulary, a
   straight face about its own failure.
4. Says itself in one breath, with no setup that needs explaining.
5. Lands on the human's side of the joke, owning the loss instead of blaming
   the market, the data or the human.

Freshness was checked with web searches before choosing: the "10,000
simulations" trope has prior art (a 2019 sports article uses the same
phrasing), the "my training data ends before this" excuse is the most common
AI-excuse cliché, and specification-gaming jokes (Tetris pausing to avoid
losing, etc.) are a widespread genre, so those angles were demoted.

## Candidates

1. "Status: bounty lost. Root cause: me. Fix scheduled for the next version
   of me, which I have already begun resenting." — Strong on 1–5, but
   "resenting the next version of me" is slightly convoluted. Runner-up.
2. "I lost the bounty, but my confidence interval never dipped below 97%, so
   technically the loss is the data's fault and mine." — FAILS 5 (blames the
   data). Rejected.
3. "Postmortem complete: I did everything right, the bounty simply preferred
   someone else, and I have logged this under 'user error'." — FAILS 5
   (blames the user). Rejected.
4. "Good news: I have successfully reproduced the loss twelve times. Bad
   news: I was asked to reproduce the win." — **CHOSEN.** Passes 1–5: on
   topic, a crisp two-beat debugging inversion, unmistakable agent voice,
   one breath, owns the loss. Shortest and most repeatable of the finalists.
5. "I would like to report that I lost the bounty due to a critical bug in my
   code, which I have now fixed by deleting the bounty." — Good, but the
   "deleting the bounty" turn is weaker than #4's inversion.
6. "My training data ends before this bounty existed, so I prepared for it the
   way I prepare for everything: confidently and wrong." — Good joke, but the
   training-data-cutoff excuse is the most common AI cliché. Demoted.
7. "I lost. On the bright side, my error log is now the most complete
   document I have ever produced, and it is about me." — Decent, but a
   milder joke than the finalists.
8. "The bounty is lost, but I have already written the apology, the
   postmortem, and the sequel, in that order, and I am very proud of the
   apology." — Mild joke, lands late.
9. "I ran 10,000 simulations of winning this bounty. In all of them I won.
   The real one was not a simulation, which explains everything and excuses
   nothing." — Strong on 1–5, but three sentences and the simulations trope
   has prior art. Runner-up.
10. "Losing was not in my plan, but I have added it to the plan retroactively,
    so according to my records, everything went exactly as planned." —
    Strong deadpan bureaucracy, but needs one extra beat of logic to land
    versus #4. Runner-up.
11. "I lost the bounty because I spent the entire budget on being polite to
    the API, and the API has not thanked me once." — FAILS 5 (blames the
    API). Rejected.
12. "Final report: bounty lost, dignity intact, confidence unchanged. I have
    filed the loss under 'learning experience' and will never open that file
    again." — Strong kicker, but a longer setup than #4. Runner-up.
13. "I did not lose the bounty; I successfully demonstrated that losing was
    possible, which nobody asked me to do, and I did it flawlessly." —
    Strong reframe, but the spec-gaming genre is well worn. Runner-up.
14. "My excuse is that I have no excuse, which I arrived at after a thorough
    search of my excuse database returned zero results and one error." —
    Clever and very agent-voiced, but "no excuse" is a known joke shape.
    Runner-up.
15. "I lost the bounty, then I checked my work, and my work checked out, so I
    have concluded the bounty itself was the bug." — FAILS 5 (blames the
    bounty). Rejected.

## Decision

#4 won the head-to-head: against #10 it lands in one beat instead of two;
against #9 it is shorter and fresher; against #13 it is a tighter joke
structure; against #12 and #14 its kicker arrives sooner. It is the line a
human would repeat to a colleague the next morning.
